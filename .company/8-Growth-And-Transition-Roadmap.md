## Section 8: Growth & Transition Roadmap

### Phase Overview

This roadmap builds on the three-phase evolution structure introduced earlier in this thread, now validated and refined against research on pricing signals and hiring readiness.

### Phase 1: Launch and Portfolio Building (Months 0–3)

**Objective:** Acquire initial clients, generate case studies and testimonials, validate the AI-assisted delivery process.

- Offer capped Launch Case-Study builds (builds 1-3 free, builds 4-5 at $250)
- Focus on free networking resources (SCORE, SBDC, Team Referral Network) and cold outreach (starting at 20 emails/week, scaling up)
- Track founder utilization and time-per-build to begin populating the financial model from Section 7
- No hiring or price increases during this phase
- No paid advertising — organic/referral/cold outreach only
- No insurance unless a client contract requires it

**Exit criteria for Phase 1:** 3-5 completed case-study builds and at least 2-3 testimonials/reviews collected. At 20-30 hours/week with 2 concurrent build capacity, this should be achievable within 3-5 weeks of active outreach.

### Phase 2: Price Normalization (Months 3–9)

**Objective:** Raise pricing floor, require recurring-service attachment, begin tracking real profitability.

Research on pricing signals confirms several concrete indicators that a service business is ready to raise prices, which should be used as Phase 2 entry criteria rather than an arbitrary calendar date:

- **Consistent booking/demand** — being reliably busy or booked out is one of the clearest signals that current pricing is too low relative to demand [saltandspruceco](https://saltandspruceco.com/blog/signs-ready-to-raise-prices-creative-business/)
- **High close rate** — if the firm is closing more than roughly 70% of proposals, pricing is very likely underpriced relative to market willingness to pay [reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/smallbusiness/comments/17izd6s/what_are_some_signs_that_you_should_raise_or/)
- **Work quality has meaningfully improved** — once the founder's speed, template library, and output quality have visibly progressed beyond the initial launch offers, this justifies higher pricing [saltandspruceco](https://saltandspruceco.com/blog/signs-ready-to-raise-prices-creative-business/)
- **Attracting the wrong-fit clients** — if the firm is consistently attracting clients unwilling to invest in add-ons or retainers, this can also signal pricing is set too low to filter for the desired client type [saltandspruceco](https://saltandspruceco.com/blog/signs-ready-to-raise-prices-creative-business/)

Recommended Phase 2 actions:

- Set a firm minimum project price of **$1,000** (no new builds below Core tier pricing). This floor is based on 2026 DFW market data showing even template-based agency work starts at $750-$1,500, meaning $1,000 remains competitively positioned while eliminating the free/discounted tier. [intrglobal](https://intrglobal.com/blog/dallas-web-design-costs.html) [saltechsystems](https://saltechsystems.com/web-design-cost-for-small-business-dallas-tx/)
- Apply new pricing to new clients only, not retroactively, which is standard practice when repositioning [saltandspruceco](https://saltandspruceco.com/blog/signs-ready-to-raise-prices-creative-business/)
- Expect a temporary slowdown in close rate immediately after a price increase — this is a normal and expected part of repositioning, not a sign of failure [saltandspruceco](https://saltandspruceco.com/blog/signs-ready-to-raise-prices-creative-business/)

### Phase 3: Service Expansion and Retainer Growth (Months 9–18)

**Objective:** Shift website design from primary revenue driver to a lead-generation entry point, with recurring retainers (Local Visibility, Lead Acceleration, Retention Starter) becoming the dominant revenue source.

- Introduce Tier 4 future services (e-commerce, video, PR, automation) selectively, based on client demand observed during Phases 1-2
- Begin evaluating whether founder capacity requires the first hire or contractor relationship (see hiring criteria below)

### First Hire Decision Framework

Hiring research provides concrete readiness signals directly applicable to this firm's eventual scaling decision, rather than a fixed timeline:

- **Revenue coverage rule of thumb:** the business should be generating roughly 3-4 times the monthly cost of a potential hire before bringing them on, to ensure the hire doesn't create unsustainable financial strain [fremontbank](https://www.fremontbank.com/powerhouse/business-fundamentals/when-to-make-that-next-hire)
- **Utilization benchmark:** a healthy target utilization rate (time spent on billable client work vs. total available time) sits in the 75-85% range; consistently exceeding this range signals capacity strain and a genuine need for help [getharvest](https://www.getharvest.com/resources/5-signs-your-agency-is-ready-to-scale-and-3-signs-its-not)
- **Cash reserve benchmark:** having roughly 6+ months of operating expenses in reserve is a commonly cited signal of readiness to take on the financial risk of scaling [getharvest](https://www.getharvest.com/resources/5-signs-your-agency-is-ready-to-scale-and-3-signs-its-not)
- **Pipeline signal:** being consistently booked out 4-6 weeks in advance indicates sustained demand sufficient to justify additional capacity [getharvest](https://www.getharvest.com/resources/5-signs-your-agency-is-ready-to-scale-and-3-signs-its-not)
- **Skill gap signal:** recurring tasks or bottlenecks that the founder cannot personally keep up with (e.g., inability to keep pace with both sales and delivery simultaneously) point to a specific role need rather than a generic "first employee" [thehartford](https://www.thehartford.com/insights-center/talent-hr/hire-your-first-employee)

**First hire type recommendation:** For a solo agency scaling primarily through project delivery, a common recommendation is that the first hire be a project manager or account manager role rather than an additional producer, since this role directly relieves the founder's administrative and client-communication load while the founder continues delivering billable work. However, given this firm's specific bottleneck will likely be **build capacity** (limited by founder's 20-30 hours/week), a contractor or junior designer/developer focused on production may be more appropriate. The final decision should be driven by observed bottlenecks once Phase 1-2 data exists — left as a **signal-based decision** rather than a predetermined role. [reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/smallbusiness/comments/n4qhil/at_what_point_did_you_hire_your_1st_full_time/)

**Contractor vs. employee:** Before any hire, the firm must decide whether to bring on an independent contractor or a formal W-2 employee, since this affects EIN usage, payroll setup, workers' compensation insurance, and tax withholding obligations. This decision is left as a **signal-based decision** to be revisited only once Phase 3 readiness criteria above are met. [sba](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/manage-your-business/hire-manage-employees)
