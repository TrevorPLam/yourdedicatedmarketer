### Client Contracts and Scope Protection

Multiple independent sources converge on the same essential components for a web design contract, confirming these are non-negotiable inclusions rather than optional extras:

- **Scope of work** — a complete, itemized list of everything included (page count, features, integrations, revision rounds) [elementor](https://elementor.com/blog/web-design-contract/)
- **Payment terms** — structured as deposit, milestone payment, and final payment, with a defined hourly rate for any work outside scope [youtube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jRerueLiY08)
- **Review and approval process** — a specific number of days allotted for client feedback on each round, preventing indefinite delays [webflow](https://webflow.com/blog/web-design-contract)
- **Termination clause** — conditions under which either party can exit, and financial consequences for doing so [elementor](https://elementor.com/blog/web-design-contract/)
- **Copyright/IP protection clause** — addressing ownership of the final deliverable and protection against the client using unlicensed assets [webflow](https://webflow.com/blog/web-design-contract)
- **Breach and cure period** — commonly a 30-day window for the accused party to resolve any alleged breach before further action [elementor](https://elementor.com/blog/web-design-contract/)
- **Governing jurisdiction** — should specify a legal jurisdiction local to the founder (Texas) for any disputes [elementor](https://elementor.com/blog/web-design-contract/)

### Founder Capacity Planning

**Founder availability:** Part-time, 20-30 hours/week, operating alongside other commitments.

**Concurrent project capacity:** 2 client builds at a time.

Freelance capacity planning guidance is directly relevant given this is a solo operation. The core principle from capacity-planning research: new solo operators should start with a maximum of 1-2 concurrent client projects until proven capacity is established, and should reserve dedicated weekly hours for business development (sales, marketing, admin) separate from client delivery hours.

Key planning inputs applied to this firm:

- At 20-30 hours/week, approximately 10 hours/week is allocated to business-building activity (outreach, networking, admin, content), leaving 10-20 hours/week for active client delivery
- 2 concurrent builds at 7-14 days each means roughly 4-8 builds per month at full capacity, though realistic throughput accounting for discovery calls, revisions, and polish windows is likely 2-4 builds per month
- Gaps in the schedule during ramp-up should be intentionally used for outreach and business development rather than treated as idle time
- The 3-5 case-study build target for Phase 1 should be achievable within 3-5 weeks at this capacity
