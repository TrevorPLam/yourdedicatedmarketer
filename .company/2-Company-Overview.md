### Legal Structure — Single-Member LLC (Decided)

The legal structure has been decided as a **Single-Member LLC**, filed via Certificate of Formation (Form 205) with the Texas Secretary of State. The $300 one-time filing fee is funded from the first client payment, per the revenue reinvestment funding model. The founder serves as registered agent (no additional cost). For reference, the comparison with Sole Proprietorship that informed this decision is retained below.

**Option A: Sole Proprietorship**
- Simplest and lowest-cost structure to start; no state filing fee to establish the business itself [uschamber](https://www.uschamber.com/co/start/strategy/sole-proprietorship-vs-llc)
- No legal separation between the owner and the business — personal assets (savings, vehicle, home) are exposed to business debts or lawsuits [wise](https://wise.com/us/blog/llc-vs-sole-proprietorship-texas)
- If operating under the name "Your Dedicated Marketer" rather than the founder's personal legal name, Texas law requires filing an Assumed Name Certificate (DBA) with the county clerk in Tarrant County; this does not go through the Secretary of State for a sole proprietor [1800accountant](https://1800accountant.com/blog/start-dba-texas)
- DBA filing cost is nominal (approximately $25 in most Texas counties, though this can vary by county clerk's office) [chamberofcommerce](https://www.chamberofcommerce.org/dba/texas/)
- An EIN is still recommended even without employees, to separate business and personal tax identification [tailorbrands](https://www.tailorbrands.com/blog/sole-proprietorship-texas)

**Option B: Single-Member LLC**
- Creates a distinct legal entity separate from the founder, providing liability protection for personal assets against business debts and claims [wolterskluwer](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en/expert-insights/singlemember-llc-vs-sole-proprietorship)
- Requires filing a Certificate of Formation (Form 205) with the Texas Secretary of State, with a filing fee of $300 [sos.texas](https://www.sos.texas.gov/corp/forms/205_boc.doc)
- Requires appointing a registered agent with a physical Texas address to receive legal documents — this can be the founder or a paid registered agent service (typically $0–$300+/year if outsourced) [texasregisteredagents](https://www.texasregisteredagents.com/texas-llc/)
- An Operating Agreement is strongly recommended even for single-member LLCs, though not legally mandated [wise](https://wise.com/us/blog/llc-vs-sole-proprietorship-texas)
- An EIN from the IRS is necessary for tax purposes and to open a business bank account under the LLC [texasregisteredagents](https://www.texasregisteredagents.com/texas-llc/)
- If the LLC operates under a name different from its registered legal name, it would still need to file a DBA (Assumed Name Certificate), at a lower state filing cost of $25 [chamberofcommerce](https://www.chamberofcommerce.org/dba/texas/)

### Comparison Summary

| Factor | Sole Proprietorship | Single-Member LLC |
|---|---|---|
| Personal liability protection | None [wise](https://wise.com/us/blog/llc-vs-sole-proprietorship-texas) | Yes [wolterskluwer](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en/expert-insights/singlemember-llc-vs-sole-proprietorship) |
| Setup cost | Low (DBA ~$25 if needed) [1800accountant](https://1800accountant.com/blog/start-dba-texas) | $300 state filing fee [sos.texas](https://www.sos.texas.gov/corp/forms/205_boc.doc) |
| Ongoing complexity | Minimal | Registered agent, operating agreement recommended [wise](https://wise.com/us/blog/llc-vs-sole-proprietorship-texas) |
| Tax filing | Simplest, reported on personal return [uschamber](https://www.uschamber.com/co/start/strategy/sole-proprietorship-vs-llc) | Flexible; can still default to personal return taxation [wolterskluwer](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en/expert-insights/singlemember-llc-vs-sole-proprietorship) |
| Perceived credibility with clients | Lower | Generally higher |

Given that the business model involves signed client contracts, recurring billing relationships, and use of AI-generated code/deliverables — all of which carry some liability exposure — the LLC structure was selected. The $300 filing fee is a one-time cost (no annual report fee in Texas), and the franchise tax is $0 for entities with annualized revenue below $2.65 million (2026 threshold), though a Public Information Report must still be filed by May 15 each year. [comptroller.texas](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/franchise/)
